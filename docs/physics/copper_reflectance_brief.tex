\section*{1.\quad Conductivity}

Room-temperature conductivity of copper (universal across grades):
\[
  \sigma_\mathrm{RT} = 5.96\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{S/m}
\]

At cryogenic temperature $T$ with residual resistance ratio $\mathrm{RRR}$,
Matthiessen's rule gives:
\[
  \frac{1}{\sigma(T,\mathrm{RRR})}
  = \frac{1}{\sigma_\mathrm{impurity}} + \frac{1}{\sigma_\mathrm{phonon}(T)}
\]
\[
  \sigma_\mathrm{impurity} = \mathrm{RRR}\times\sigma_\mathrm{RT},
  \qquad
  \sigma_\mathrm{phonon}(T) \approx \sigma_\mathrm{RT}\,\frac{273\,\mathrm{K}}{T}
  \quad (T \gtrsim 50\ \mathrm{K})
\]

At $T = 4\ \mathrm{K}$, phonon scattering is frozen out and
$\sigma(4\,\mathrm{K}) \simeq \mathrm{RRR}\times\sigma_\mathrm{RT}$.

\section*{2.\quad Drude scattering time}

The electron scattering time $\tau$ is set by the DC conductivity
and free-electron parameters of copper:
\[
  \tau = \frac{\sigma_\mathrm{DC}\, m_e}{n_e\, e^2}
\]

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
  $n_e = 8.49\times10^{28}\ \mathrm{m}^{-3}$ & free electron density \\
  $m_e = 9.109\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$    & electron mass \\
  $e   = 1.602\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$     & electron charge
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For OFHC Cu ($\mathrm{RRR}=100$) at $4\ \mathrm{K}$:
$\tau \approx 2.5\ \mathrm{ps}$.

\section*{3.\quad Drude AC conductivity}

The frequency-dependent (AC) conductivity:
\[
  \sigma(\omega) = \frac{\sigma_\mathrm{DC}}{1 - i\omega\tau}
\]

\section*{4.\quad Complex wave vector (Griffiths \S9.4)}

For a plane wave in a conductor with real part of AC conductivity
$\sigma_r = \mathrm{Re}[\sigma(\omega)]$, the complex wave vector
$\tilde{k} = k + i\kappa$ satisfies (Griffiths eqs.\ 9.126):
\[
  k = \frac{\omega}{c}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0\mu_0}{2}}
      \sqrt{\,\sqrt{1 + \!\left(\frac{\sigma_r}{\varepsilon_0\omega}\right)^{\!2}} + 1\,}
  \qquad
  \kappa = \frac{\omega}{c}\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0\mu_0}{2}}
      \sqrt{\,\sqrt{1 + \!\left(\frac{\sigma_r}{\varepsilon_0\omega}\right)^{\!2}} - 1\,}
\]

The complex refractive index:
\[
  \tilde{n} = n + ik_\mathrm{opt} = \frac{c\,\tilde{k}}{\omega}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  n = \frac{c\,k}{\omega},\quad k_\mathrm{opt} = \frac{c\,\kappa}{\omega}
\]

\section*{5.\quad Reflectance}

Normal-incidence Fresnel reflectance from vacuum into the conductor:
\[
  \boxed{R = \left|\frac{\tilde{n}-1}{\tilde{n}+1}\right|^2
           = \frac{(n-1)^2 + k_\mathrm{opt}^2}{(n+1)^2 + k_\mathrm{opt}^2}}
\]

Absorptance: $D = 1 - R$.

\section*{6.\quad Hagen-Rubens limit}

In the limit $\omega\tau \ll 1$ (low frequency), $\sigma(\omega)\to\sigma_\mathrm{DC}$
and $n \approx k_\mathrm{opt} \gg 1$, giving:
\[
  D = 1 - R \approx 2\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_0\omega}{\sigma_\mathrm{DC}}}
\]

This limit breaks down above the scattering frequency:
\[
  f_\mathrm{break} = \frac{1}{2\pi\tau}
  \approx \frac{n_e e^2}{2\pi\,m_e\,\sigma_\mathrm{DC}}
\]

For OFHC Cu ($\mathrm{RRR}=100$) at $4\ \mathrm{K}$:
$f_\mathrm{break}\approx 64\ \mathrm{GHz}$.
BBRsim spans $50\ \mathrm{GHz}$--$20\ \mathrm{THz}$;
Hagen-Rubens is inadequate for essentially the entire range at
cryogenic temperatures.
